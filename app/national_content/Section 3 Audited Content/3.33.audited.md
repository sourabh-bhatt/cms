# Transfer of Title and Property Rights

<!-- Removed below para  -->

<!-- When you're learning about **property rights**, it's crucial to understand how the **transfer of title** works. This concept relates directly to how ownership of real estate changes hands. There are two main ways this can happen: -->

In property rights, the transfer of title refers to how ownership of real estate changes hands. There are two primary methods of transfer:

- **Voluntarily**, as in a sale
- **Involuntarily**, through situations like foreclosure or eminent domain

## Alienation: The Transfer of Property Ownership

The term **"alienation"** is key here. It's a fancy way of saying the transfer of property ownership. However, there's an important point about alienation to remember: typically, the law does not favor any restrictions that prevent the transfer of property. Exceptions are made only if these restrictions are reasonable and do not last forever.

## The Bundle of Legal Rights and Transfer Limitations

<!-- Removed You, your -->

One of the fundamental rights of a property owner is the ability to transfer or dispose of property to someone else. This is part of what's often referred to as the **"bundle of legal rights"** associated with property ownership.

An important principle to remember is that no one can transfer more rights than they actually possess. In other words, the ability to transfer property is limited to the rights held by the owner.

## The Role of Title in Property Transfer

To ensure this principle is upheld, the role of a **title** comes into play. A title is proof of ownership in a piece of land. This could be in the form of a **deed**, which serves as evidence of property ownership.

Before any transfer takes place, a **title search** is conducted. This is a detailed examination to determine the exact rights attached to the property, ensuring that only the rights legitimately owned are transferred.
