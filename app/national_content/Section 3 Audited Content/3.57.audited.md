# Termination of Easements

<!-- Understanding how **easements** can end is crucial for your knowledge in the realm of property rights. Imagine you're handling a property that either benefits from or is burdened by an easement. Knowing when and how these easements come to an end can significantly affect your strategy and advice. -->

Knowing how **easements** terminate affects property rights. Properties may benefit from or be burdened by easements. When and how these easements end affects property strategy.

## Easements Created Out of Necessity

- Easements created out of necessity, such as for access to a landlocked property, naturally conclude when the need for them no longer exists.
<!-- - *Example:* If a new road provides alternative access, the easement may no longer be necessary. -->
- Example: If a new road provides alternative access, the easement may no longer be necessary.

## Easements Appurtenant

Easements that are **appurtenant** benefit a particular piece of land (the **dominant estate**) and apply to another piece (the **servient estate**). They can end in several ways:

1. **Merger**
   - When the dominant and servient estates become owned by the same person or entity.
   - Since the easement's purpose is to serve as a benefit or burden between different parties, ownership merging dissolves the easement.

2. **Formal Release**
   - The owner of the dominant estate explicitly gives up their easement rights, typically in writing.
   - This is a formal relinquishment of the easement's benefit.

3. **Abandonment**
   - Involves both ceasing to use the easement and clearly demonstrating the intention never to use it again.
   - Both lack of use and intent to abandon must be evident.

4. **Destruction of Servient Estate**
   - If the land subject to the easement (servient estate) is destroyed by a natural disaster or an **act of God**, the easement can be extinguished.
   - If the land no longer exists or cannot be used as dictated by the easement, the easement ends.

5. **Legal Action (Quiet Title Suit)**
   - A court may decide that the easement is no longer valid or enforceable under certain circumstances.
