# Title by Estoppel in Property Ownership

<!-- When studying **property ownership** in Virginia, you need to understand **title by estoppel**. -->
**Title by estoppel** represents a fundamental concept in Virginia property ownership.

## What is Title by Estoppel?

Title by estoppel happens when someone conveys property they do not own. Later, that person acquires title to the property.

Here is how it works:

- Person A does not own a property
- Person A sells the property to Person B anyway
- Person B receives a deed (even though Person A had no right to sell)
- Later, Person A actually acquires legal title to the property
- The law automatically transfers Person A's new title to Person B

This is called "estoppel by deed."

## Virginia Law on Title by Estoppel

Virginia recognizes the doctrine of estoppel by deed. This means:

- The original buyer (Person B) gets good title automatically
- Person A cannot claim the property back
- Person A is "estopped" from denying the validity of the original deed

## Example Scenario

Example: John sells Mary a house he does not own. Mary records her deed. Two years later, John inherits the same house from his uncle. Under Virginia law, Mary automatically becomes the true owner. John cannot claim the house back.

## Why This Doctrine Exists

Title by estoppel protects:

- Innocent buyers who rely on deeds
- The stability of property transactions
- Good faith purchasers

This doctrine encourages people to be careful when making property conveyances.
