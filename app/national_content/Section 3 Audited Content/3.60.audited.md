# Easements in Gross

<!-- When you're delving into **property rights**, one important concept you'll encounter is **easements in gross**. Think of an easement in gross as a special permission that gives someone the limited right to use another person's land. Unlike other types of easements, easements in gross are tied to specific individuals, *not* the land itself. This means they're a bit like a personal privilege granted to someone. -->

**Easements in gross** provide limited rights to use another person's land. Unlike other types of easements, easements in gross are tied to specific individuals, *not* the land itself.

## Distinction Between Easements in Gross and Easements Appurtenant

<!-- A key point to grasp is the difference between **easements in gross** and **easements appurtenant**: -->

The difference between **easements in gross** and **easements appurtenant**:

- **Easements in gross** involve a **servient estate**—the land being used—but *no dominant estate* (land benefiting from the easement).
- **Easements appurtenant** involve both a **servient estate** and a **dominant estate**.

The big difference lies in **transferability**:

- With easements in gross, the burden of the easement does *not* automatically transfer if the land is sold.
- The benefit of the easement usually cannot be passed on by the holder of the easement.

## Exception: Commercial Easements in Gross

<!-- There is an important exception for **commercial easements in gross**. These easements can be transferred and assigned, meaning: -->

**Commercial easements in gross** provide an exception. These easements can be transferred and assigned, meaning:

- If a property has a commercial easement in gross (for example, for cable, telephone, or gas services), that easement remains with the property even when it changes hands.
