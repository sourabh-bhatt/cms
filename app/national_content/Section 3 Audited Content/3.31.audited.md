# Understanding Estates in Land

<!-- When you're delving into the realm of **property rights**, it's essential to grasp the concept of **estates in land**. Think of an estate as a way to describe the level, amount, nature, and bounds of your ownership stake in real property. It's a nuanced way of saying that anyone holding title to real property isn't outright owning the land but rather owns an "estate" or interest in the land. This interest is what can be bought and sold. -->

Estates in land describe the level, nature, and extent of ownership in real property. Holding title to land does not mean absolute ownership of the land itself but ownership of an estate or interest in it. This interest can be bought and sold.

## Categories of Estates in Land

<!-- There are four broad categories of estates you should familiarize yourself with: -->

There are four broad categories of estates:

1. **Freehold Estates**

   - This type is most closely associated with the traditional concept of land ownership.
   - It involves having an indefinite duration of ownership interest in the property.
   - There's no set end date, giving a sense of permanence and stability to the interest in the land.

2. **Future Estates**

   - These involve ownership interests that are set to begin in the future.
   - It's like having a stake in what's to come, where the interest is established now but the actual possession or full benefits kick in at a later date.

3. **Non-Freehold (Leasehold) Estates**

   - These relate to tenancies and leases.
   - Non-freehold estates represent a temporary interest in the property.
   - Certain rights and obligations exist for the duration of the lease or tenancy.
   - This interest has a clear expiry date.

4. **Statutory Estates**
   - Created by law, these estates are defined and governed by local legislation.
   - They might include rights granted by laws related to marriage, debts, or other specific legal circumstances impacting property rights.
