# Title by Estoppel: Understanding This Legal Principle

**Title by Estoppel**, also known as **deed by estoppel**, is a legal concept in real estate transactions. It applies when someone conveys property they do not yet own, but later acquires ownership of that property.

## How Title by Estoppel Works

- When someone conveys property they do not yet own, but later acquires ownership, the title automatically passes to the grantee.
- This principle means the grantor cannot deny the validity of their deed, even if they did not own the property when they signed it.

## Importance in Real Estate

Protects the investment by ensuring a clear title, even if the grantor did not own the property at the time of the original conveyance.

Maintains the integrity of property acquisitions by holding parties accountable for their deeds and conveyances.

**Example:**

John sells property to Mary in January, but John does not actually own the property yet. In March, John inherits the property. Under title by estoppel, Mary automatically receives ownership in March when John acquires it. John cannot claim he never validly conveyed the property to Mary.
