# Understanding Wills and Their Role in Estate Planning

<!-- When you think of planning for the future, especially in terms of what happens to your possessions and assets after you pass away, understanding **wills** becomes crucial.  -->

The individual who creates a will is known as a **testator**. A will is essentially a roadmap for distributing a person's **estate**, which includes both real and personal assets, following their death. However, it's important to remember that a will holds no legal power until the testator dies.

## The Probate Process

Upon the death of the testator, the will must be presented to a local court to undergo the **probate process**. This process:

- Validates the will, ensuring it isn't a forgery.
- Confirms the testator was in a sound state of mind when creating the will.
- Checks if the **executor** or **personal representative**—the person tasked with distributing the estate as per the will—is fulfilling the testator's wishes and settling any debts.

## Common Types of Wills

Let's explore some common types of wills that come across:

1. **Simple Will:**

   - Provides a straightforward plan for passing on assets directly to the beneficiaries without any complex legal structures.

2. **Testamentary Trust Will:**

   - Establishes one or more trusts to manage the testator's property after their death.
   - Offers more control over how assets are distributed.

3. **Holographic Will:**

   - Handwritten by the testator and not witnessed.
   - Virginia recognizes holographic wills if they are entirely in the testator's handwriting and signed by the testator.

4. **Pour-Over Will:**

   - Directs some or all of the testator's assets into a previously established living trust.
   - Seamlessly transfers ownership upon the testator's death.

5. **Oral Will (Nuncupative Will):**

   - Expressed verbally.
   - Virginia does not recognize oral wills except in very limited circumstances for military personnel in actual service.
   - Generally covers personal property but cannot be used to transfer real estate.

6. **Living Will:**
   - Not to be confused with a living trust.
   - Does not distribute assets but instructs medical professionals on life support decisions if the individual is terminally ill or incapacitated.
   - Active only during the person's lifetime.
