# Easement by Necessity in Property Rights

<!-- Updated with the new version -->

An easement by necessity arises when a specific use of land is legally required, even without an explicit agreement or reservation. Unlike other easements created through formal agreements, it is established by law.

## Understanding Easement by Necessity

- An easement by necessity occurs when a property is **landlocked**, meaning it has no direct access to a public road or pathway.
- In such cases, the owner must access or cross another person's property to reach their landlocked property.
- This legal easement exists solely because of the underlying need for access.

## Key Points to Remember

- The easement by necessity is not based on a formal agreement but is imposed by law.
- If the necessity for the easement ceases to exist—such as when an alternative route is developed or the property is no longer landlocked—the easement by necessity terminates.

## Example

Imagine owning a parcel of land completely surrounded by other properties with no direct road access. To reach your property, you must cross a neighbor's land. In this case, an **easement by necessity** allows you legal access across that neighbor's property to reach your own.
