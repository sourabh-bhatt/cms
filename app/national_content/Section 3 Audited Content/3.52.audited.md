# Understanding Future Estates in Property Rights

<!-- When learning about property rights, it's crucial to understand the concept of **Future Estates**. Imagine you own a piece of land, but you don't have the right to use it or live on it just yet. That's what we mean by a *Future Estate* – it's an ownership interest in property that you're certain to fully own and possess at some point in the future, but not right now. -->

A **Future Estate** is an ownership interest in property that will be fully owned and possessed at some point in the future, but not currently.

## Present Ownership Interest vs. Future Estate

<!-- With a future estate, you have an ownership interest in the property. You own it, but you don't have the present right to occupy or use it. This situation is temporary. A day will come when your Future Estate transforms into a present estate, granting you full ownership and possession. This could be: -->

With a future estate, ownership exists without present rights to occupy or use the property. This temporary situation ends when the Future Estate transforms into a present estate, granting full ownership and possession. This could be:

- A **Fee Simple Absolute**, where you own the property outright with no limitations.
- A **Life Estate**, where you own and use the property for the duration of someone's life.

## Principal Types of Future Estates

The two main types of Future Estates are the **Remainder Estate** and the **Reversion Estate**.

1. **Remainder Estate**

   - This activates after a life estate ends.
   - Example: If a property is given to one person for their lifetime, then specified to pass to another person afterward, that second person holds a Remainder Estate.
   - They become the owners once the life estate concludes.

2. **Reversion Estate**
   <!-- - This involves the property eventually going back to the original owner or the original owner's heirs.
   - Example: If someone grants a property to another person for their lifetime, without specifying who it goes to afterward, once the life estate ends, the property reverts to the grantor or their heirs. -->
   - This involves property returning to the original owner or their heirs.
   - Example: If someone grants property to another person for their lifetime without specifying who receives it afterward, the property reverts to the grantor or their heirs when the life estate ends.
