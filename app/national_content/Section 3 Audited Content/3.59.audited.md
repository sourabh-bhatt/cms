# Joint Tenancy and Changes in Property Ownership

<!-- When you're learning about **property rights**, it's crucial to understand how different **ownership structures** impact the rights and abilities of the owners. Specifically, let's delve into **joint tenancy**, a popular form of holding title to property, and what happens when changes occur. -->

Different **ownership structures** impact the rights and abilities of property owners. **Joint tenancy** is a common form of holding title to property.

## Understanding Joint Tenancy

<!-- - In a **joint tenancy**, you and the other owners hold the property together under **equal rights**. -->
- In a **joint tenancy**, owners hold the property together under **equal rights**.
- Joint tenancy can end in specific situations:
  - **Mutual agreement**: If all parties decide, they can terminate the joint tenancy.
  - **Sale of interest**: When one joint tenant sells their ownership interest.

## Rights and Limitations of Joint Tenants

<!-- - As a joint tenant, you **cannot pass on your ownership interest through a will**.
- However, you **do have the right to sell your interest**. -->
- Joint tenants **cannot pass on ownership interest through a will**.
- However, joint tenants **have the right to sell their interest**.

## Impact of Selling a Joint Tenant's Interest

<!-- - When you sell your share, the buyer does **not** become a joint tenant because they cannot meet the required **unities**. -->
- When a joint tenant sells their share, the buyer does **not** become a joint tenant because they cannot meet the required **unities**.
- The **unities** are specific conditions necessary to maintain a joint tenancy. All four unities must be present:
  - **Unity of time**: All owners must acquire their interest at the same time
  - **Unity of title**: All owners must acquire their interest through the same deed
  - **Unity of interest**: All owners must have equal ownership shares
  - **Unity of possession**: All owners must have equal rights to use the entire property
- Since the buyer cannot meet these unities, they become a **tenant-in-common** with the remaining owners.

## Tenant-in-Common vs. Joint Tenant

- A **tenant-in-common** owns a specific percentage share of the property.
- Unlike joint tenants, tenants-in-common **do not have survivorship rights**.
- Tenants-in-common **can use the entire property but cannot exclude other owners**.

<!-- Understanding these distinctions is essential to navigating property ownership and recognizing how rights shift when ownership or title status changes. -->

These distinctions affect property ownership and how rights shift when ownership or title status changes.
