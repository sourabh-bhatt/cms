# Understanding Joint Tenancy and the Right of Survivorship

## How Ownership Transfers in Joint Tenancy

In a **joint tenancy**, the rule called **Right of Survivorship** explains what happens when a co-owner dies. Under this rule, the deceased owner’s share does not pass through a will or probate. Instead, that share is automatically transferred to the surviving joint tenants, increasing their ownership share equally.

## Ownership After Survivorship

Over time, as other joint tenants pass away, the last surviving tenant becomes the sole owner of the property. This is known as holding a **title in severalty**, which means complete ownership by one person (**sole ownership**). At this stage, the sole owner has full control and can:

- Transfer the property to someone else through a deed during their lifetime.
- Decide who will inherit the property after their death

## Important Considerations in Joint Tenancy

- A joint tenant **cannot** pass their interest in the property to another person through a will.
- This unique feature of joint tenancy makes it important to carefully consider how the property title is taken.
- Proper planning is necessary to decide the future of the estate and how the property will be managed after an owner’s death.
