# Easements and Property Rights

## Understanding Easements

<!-- When you're learning about **property rights**, one important concept is the notion of an **easement**. When learning about **property rights**, understanding the concept of an **easement** is necessary. An easement refers to the right someone might have to use a portion of another person's property for a specified purpose. This applies in situations where property use and access are concerned.

## Creation of Easements Through Written Consent

<!-- An easement can be created through **written consent**. This type is called an **express easement**. An easement can be created through **written consent**. This type is called an **express easement**. This occurs when the property owner formally agrees, in writing, to grant someone else the right to use their property. This is *not* a transfer of ownership; rather, it is a right to access or utilize the property in a certain way.

## Example Scenario: Landlocked Property

<!-- To fully grasp this concept, consider the following example: -->

Example:

- A property is **landlocked**, meaning it has no direct access to a public road.
- The only way to reach the road is by crossing a neighboring property.
- The landlocked property owner negotiates an easement with the neighbor.
- This easement grants the landlocked owner the right to pass through the neighbor's property.
- The agreement is documented in writing, establishing a legal right for access.

## Legal Implications of Easements

- Easements created by written consent are **legally binding**.
- They typically remain in effect even if the property is sold, unless the agreement specifies otherwise.
- The easement should be recorded in the public records to provide proper notice to future property owners.
- Recording the easement protects the rights of the easement holder.
