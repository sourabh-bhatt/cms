# Tenancy in Common: Key Concepts in Property Co-Ownership

<!-- When you're navigating the landscape of property ownership, one key concept you need to grasp is **Tenancy in Common**. This is typically the default form of co-ownership, stepping in unless another form of tenancy is explicitly stated. -->

**Tenancy in Common** serves as the default form of co-ownership unless another form of tenancy is explicitly stated.

## Ownership Structure in Tenancy in Common

<!-- In a Tenancy in Common scenario, co-owners, also known as **co-tenants**, hold the title to the property directly in their own names and under a **fee simple interest**. This means you, alongside any co-owners, may have either equal or unequal shares in the property. However, there's a unique characteristic that unites all co-tenants: the **unity of possession**. -->

In Tenancy in Common, co-owners (**co-tenants**) hold title to the property directly in their own names under a **fee simple interest**. Co-tenants may have equal or unequal shares in the property. All co-tenants share the **unity of possession**.

### Unity of Possession

<!-- - **Unity of possession** is a fundamental principle ensuring that no co-tenant has the right to exclude another from any portion of the property.
- Regardless of individual ownership percentages, every co-tenant has an **undivided interest** in the entire property.
- Unlike ownership forms where specific sections may be assigned to different owners, in Tenancy in Common, your share is a piece of the whole property.
- This provides all co-tenants equal rights to use and enjoy the entire space. -->

- **Unity of possession** ensures that no co-tenant can exclude another from any portion of the property.
- Every co-tenant has an **undivided interest** in the entire property, regardless of ownership percentages.
- Each share represents a portion of the whole property, not specific sections.
- All co-tenants have equal rights to use and enjoy the entire space.

## Right of Survivorship and Transfer of Interest

A major point of differentiation between Tenancy in Common and other co-ownership types, like **Joint Tenancy**, revolves around the **right of survivorship**:

- In Tenancy in Common, **there is no right of survivorship**.
- If a co-tenant passes away, their share does **not** automatically transfer to the surviving co-owners.
- Instead, the deceased's share can be bequeathed to someone outside of the tenancy, according to their will.
- This flexibility allows the transfer of interest either through sale or inheritance without the constraints of survivorship.

## Comparison with Joint Tenancy

- The absence of the right of survivorship is a key difference between Tenancy in Common and Joint Tenancy.
- Tenancy in Common emphasizes the singular **unity of possession**, meaning every co-tenant has the right to utilize the entire property, irrespective of ownership percentage.
- Transferring your interest, whether by selling it during your lifetime or through a will upon death, offers a level of control not found in Joint Tenancy arrangements.

<!-- Understanding these nuances enables you to navigate property rights and ownership structures more effectively, ensuring informed decisions in property management and ownership transfers. -->

These characteristics define Tenancy in Common as a flexible form of property co-ownership.
