# Prescriptive Easement

## Understanding Prescriptive Easements

<!-- You need to understand a critical concept known as a **prescriptive easement**. Imagine you're using someone else's land—not just passing through, but really using it without their permission and in a way that's against the owner's wishes. This isn't a friendly neighborly arrangement; it's a situation where your actions are *hostile* to the landowner's interests. -->

A **prescriptive easement** occurs when someone uses another person's land without permission and against the owner's wishes. This use is *hostile* to the landowner's interests, not a friendly neighborly arrangement.

<!-- If you continue to use this piece of land **openly**, without hiding your activities, and you do so **exclusively** (meaning it's just you using this land in this way), and your use is **uninterrupted and continuous** over a certain period—this period is twenty years in Virginia—you could earn a prescriptive easement. -->

Continued land use that is **open**, **exclusive**, and **uninterrupted and continuous** over twenty years in Virginia can result in a prescriptive easement.

## Prescriptive Easement vs. Adverse Possession

<!-- It's essential to differentiate between a **prescriptive easement** and **adverse possession**. Both use adverse possession principles, but they have an important difference: -->

**Prescriptive easements** and **adverse possession** use similar principles but differ in outcome:

- **Adverse Possession**: Acquiring an *ownership interest* in the property. After meeting specific requirements, you could become the new owner of the land.

- **Prescriptive Easement**: You are *not* gaining ownership of the land. Instead, you are earning the **legal right to use** the property in a certain way, despite having no title to it.

<!-- This right of use is what's granted through a prescriptive easement, and it's a concept crucial for you to grasp, especially when advising clients on property rights and usage scenarios. -->

Prescriptive easements grant this right of use without transferring ownership.
