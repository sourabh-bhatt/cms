# Understanding Real Estate, Real Property, and Land

<!-- When you dive into the world of **property rights**, you'll come across the term **"real estate,"** which is essentially another way to say **"real property."** However, when we narrow down to the term **"land,"** we're talking about something a bit more specific. Imagine the surface of the earth, stretching out beneath and above you—that's **land**. It encompasses natural elements like trees, crops, minerals, and water. -->

The term **"real estate"** is another way to say **"real property."** **Land** refers to the surface of the earth, including natural elements like trees, crops, minerals, and water. 

<!-- Land includes these natural features. **Real property** takes it a step further. **Real property** encompasses not just the land but also any enhancements or **fixtures** added to it—it could be anything from infrastructure, developments, to houses that stand on the land. And unlike those personal items you can pick up and move with you, **real property is immovable**. -->

**Real property** encompasses land plus any enhancements or **fixtures** added to it, including infrastructure, developments, and houses. Unlike personal items, **real property is immovable**.

## Types of Real Property

<!-- Let's break down the types of real property for a clearer picture. It spans across: -->

Real property includes:

- **Land** itself
- **Improvements**: Valuable additions like buildings and infrastructure development
- **Real estate**: Essentially land plus those mentioned improvements

<!-- A common type of improvement you'll see is houses. But there are other kinds too: -->

Houses represent common improvements. Other types include:

### Fructus Naturales

- These are permanent plantings such as perennials like flowers, grasses, trees, and bushes.
- The term "Fructus Naturales" is Latin for "fruits of nature."
<!-- - Since these belong to the realm of **real property**, they can't just be uprooted and taken away without an agreement. -->
- As **real property**, they cannot be removed without an agreement.
- They differ from **personal property** in this respect.

### Fixtures

- Fixtures refer to items that once could be moved around (**personal property**) such as fences, buildings, trees, or even bricks in a wall, but have now been fixed to the real estate.
- Once these items are fastened, they transform into **real property**.

## Example Scenario: Selling Property

Example: When an owner sells their property, they can take **personal belongings** like cars, clothes, and jewelry, but the **real property**, including all **fixtures**, transfers to the new owner.

<!-- The term **"severance"** comes into play when discussing the act of removing a fixture. Through severance, a fixture can revert back to being **personal property**. However, it's crucial to remember that not all items can be detached. Until they can be severed, **fixtures remain real property**. -->

The term **"severance"** refers to removing a fixture. Through severance, a fixture can revert to **personal property**. Not all items can be detached. Until severed, **fixtures remain real property**.

<!-- Understanding these distinctions is important. This knowledge helps you identify and work with different property elements. -->

These distinctions help identify and classify different property elements.
