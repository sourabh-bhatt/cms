# Understanding Property Rights

<!-- As you delve into understanding **property rights**, imagine you are holding the power to control, enjoy, and manage a piece of the earth—whether it's a patch of land, the air above it, or even the minerals buried deep beneath. Your rights over this property are multi-dimensional. You have rights over: -->

**Property rights** grant the power to control, enjoy, and manage land, including the surface, the air above, and the minerals below. These rights are multi-dimensional and extend to:

- The surface of the land
- The air that stretches above it
- The depths underground known for mining potential
- Any easements that permit specific uses of the land
- Water rights that may apply to the property

## The Bundle of Legal Rights

Often called the "bundle of legal rights," picture this as an actual bundle of sticks—each stick representing a distinct legal privilege associated with owning property. These principal rights include:

1. **Possess** – The right to occupy and use the property.
2. **Control** – The right to determine how the property is used.
3. **Enjoy** – The right to benefit from the property without interference.
4. **Exclude** – The right to keep others from entering or using the property.
5. **Encumber** – The right to reduce ownership rights by granting interests such as a mortgage or an easement.
6. **Dispose** – The right to sell, transfer, or pass the property on through a will.

When a piece of property changes hands, these intangible rights accompany the transfer, alongside the tangible elements like the land and any structures or fixtures on it.

Each of these rights can be retained, sold, or transferred, depending on the type of ownership and specific intentions. The composition of the bundle influences property value, as these rights determine how the property may be used.

## Water Rights

Water rights are an important aspect of property ownership, particularly in areas where water is scarce. These rights determine how nearby water may be used for purposes such as farming, recreation, or domestic needs. Rules vary by state, depending on the legal doctrine applied. Virginia follows the riparian doctrine system. Key doctrines include:

- **Riparian Doctrine**: Applies when the property borders a moving body of water (like a river or stream). Owner is entitled to reasonable use of that water.
- **Littoral Rights**: Applies to properties next to stationary bodies of water (like lakes or oceans), again allowing for reasonable use.
- **Doctrine of Prior Appropriation**: Means "first in time, first in right." The first party to divert water to land holds superior rights over later users.
