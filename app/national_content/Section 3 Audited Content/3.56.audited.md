# Sole Ownership in Property Rights

<!-- When you delve into the realm of **property rights**, one key concept you'll encounter is **sole ownership**. This is when a single entity holds the **title** to a piece of real estate. Think of it as the property being owned entirely by one person or entity—hence the term "sole." Understanding the types of ownership will prepare you to handle different property transactions efficiently. -->

**Sole ownership** occurs when a single entity holds the **title** to a piece of real estate. The property is owned entirely by one person or entity.

## What is Sole Ownership?

Also known as an **estate in severalty** or **several ownership**, sole ownership means the title to the property is held exclusively by one party. This ownership can be based on various estate types, such as:

- **Fee simple**: Represents complete ownership without any limitations on inheritance.
- **Life estate**: Ownership for the duration of someone's life.

<!-- Though these terms might sound complicated at first, they essentially describe the extent and conditions of ownership. -->

These terms describe the extent and conditions of ownership.

## Who Can Hold Sole Ownership?

Sole ownership isn't limited to individual people. It can be held by:

<!-- - **Natural persons** (individuals like you and me) -->
- **Natural persons** (individuals)
- **Artificial entities** such as:
  - **Corporations** (legally known as fictitious persons)
  - **Partnerships** (groups of people joining forces in business)

Each of these can own property individually, with all the rights and responsibilities that come along with ownership.

## Transfer of Sole Ownership Upon Death

<!-- When a property under sole ownership is passed on due to the death of the owner, it typically goes through a process called **probate**. This is where the court oversees the distribution of the estate to the heirs. This process is crucial because it governs how the property transitions from one owner to the next upon death—an inevitable part of managing estate affairs. -->

When a property under sole ownership is passed on due to the owner's death, it typically goes through **probate**. The court oversees the distribution of the estate to the heirs. This process governs how property transitions from one owner to the next upon death.
