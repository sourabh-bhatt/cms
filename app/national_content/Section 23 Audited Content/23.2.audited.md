# The Civil Rights Act of 1866 and Its Impact on Housing Equality

<!-- When you're diving into the world of real estate, understanding the foundational laws that govern housing is crucial. Among these, a landmark piece of legislation that you must familiarize yourself with is the **Civil Rights Act of 1866**. -->

Real estate requires understanding foundational laws that govern housing. A landmark piece of legislation is the **Civil Rights Act of 1866**. This federal law, which is enforced through the **13th Amendment**, plays a pivotal role in ensuring equality in housing.

## Prohibition of Racial Discrimination in Property Rights

The Civil Rights Act of 1866 specifically prohibits any discrimination based on race that may prevent someone from exercising their rights related to property. These rights encompass a broad range of activities, including:

- Inheritance
- Purchase
- Lease
- Sale
- Holding
- Conveyance

of both **real** and **personal property**. Essentially, this law was a significant step forward in ensuring that all individuals, regardless of race, have equal opportunities in the housing market.

## Constitutional Significance and Enforcement

<!-- What makes this act particularly noteworthy, especially in a legal context, is its constitutional basis in the **13th Amendment** of the U.S. Constitution. This act has constitutional basis in the **13th Amendment** of the U.S. Constitution. With constitutional authority, the enforcement of this law falls under the jurisdiction of **federal courts**. Issues or disputes related to racial discrimination in housing that violate this provision are addressed at the federal level.
