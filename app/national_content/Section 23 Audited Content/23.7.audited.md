# Reasonable Accommodations in Housing Legislation

<!-- When preparing to become proficient in housing legislation, it is essential to understand the concept of **reasonable accommodations**. -->

Housing legislation includes the concept of **reasonable accommodations**. This understanding ensures that individuals with **disabilities** receive equal opportunities to use and enjoy their dwellings without facing unnecessary barriers.

## Understanding Reasonable Accommodation Requests

<!-- When someone with a disability requests an accommodation to make their living space more accessible, consider the following: -->

When someone with a disability requests an accommodation to make their living space more accessible, the following applies:

- If the accommodation does **not** result in significant **financial hardship** or fundamentally change the nature of operations, the request is considered **reasonable** and must be honored.
  - Example: A request for a reserved parking spot closer to the entrance for someone with mobility issues is a reasonable accommodation because it is necessary for the individual's equal enjoyment of their home.

<!-- - If the accommodation could create a heavy financial burden or require changes that would fundamentally alter your operations, you must engage in an **interactive process** rather than outright denying the request. -->

- If the accommodation could create a heavy financial burden or require changes that would fundamentally alter operations, providers must engage in an **interactive process** rather than outright denying the request.

## The Interactive Process

The **interactive process** involves working together in good faith with the person requesting the accommodation to find an alternative solution that:

- Meets their needs
<!-- - Does not cause **undue hardship** or excessive burden on your part -->
- Does not cause **undue hardship** or excessive burden on the provider

<!-- *Example:* If an individual requests expensive or impractical modifications, you might explore other feasible ways to achieve the desired accessibility. -->

Example: If an individual requests expensive or impractical modifications, providers might explore other feasible ways to achieve the desired accessibility.

## Grounds for Denying a Request

Each request for reasonable accommodation should be evaluated on a case-by-case basis. A request may be denied if:

- The person making the request is **not disabled**
- There is **no disability-related need** for the accommodation
- The accommodation would impose **undue financial and administrative burdens**
<!-- - The accommodation would result in a **fundamental alteration** of your operations -->
- The accommodation would result in a **fundamental alteration** of provider operations

## Key Considerations

Navigating reasonable accommodations requires balancing:

- The needs of the individual
- The capabilities and limitations of the provider
