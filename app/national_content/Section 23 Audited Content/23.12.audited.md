# Accessibility and Adaptability Standards in New Multi-Family Construction

<!-- When it comes to **new construction**, especially **multi-family dwellings**, there are crucial design and construction standards you need to understand concerning **adaptability** and **accessibility** for individuals with disabilities. -->

**New construction**, especially **multi-family dwellings**, includes design and construction standards concerning **adaptability** and **accessibility** for individuals with disabilities. New buildings must be designed to accommodate persons with disabilities. This includes requirements to make living spaces accessible for everyone.

## Accessible Entryways

- At least one entrance must be **accessible**.
- Accessibility features may include:
  - **Ramps**
  - **Wide doors**
  - Other adaptations to ensure ease of entry for persons with disabilities.

## Interior Accessibility Features

- **Control mechanisms** such as **switches** and **outlets** must be positioned at **accessible heights**.
- This ensures that all individuals, including those using wheelchairs, can easily use the building's features.
<!-- - Example: Light switches and power outlets installed at heights reachable from a wheelchair. -->
- Example: Light switches and power outlets installed at heights reachable from a wheelchair.

## Bathroom and Kitchen Design

- **Walls in bathrooms** must be **reinforced during construction** to allow for future installation of **grab bars**.
<!-- - Grab bars provide crucial support for individuals with disabilities to use the bathroom independently. -->
- Grab bars provide support for individuals with disabilities to use the bathroom independently.
- The layout of **kitchens and bathrooms** should be spacious enough to allow **wheelchair maneuverability**.
- This design helps people move around easily in important rooms.

## Multi-Floor Building Accessibility

- For buildings with **multiple floors** and **elevators**:
  - All units must comply with **accessibility standards** to ensure each level is reachable and usable by persons with disabilities.
- For buildings without elevators:
  - Only **ground-floor units** must adhere to **handicapped access requirements**.
