# 5.31 The Sheriff's Deed in Real Estate Transactions

Real estate transactions involve different types of **deeds**. The **Sheriff's Deed** represents one type. This deed shares similarities with deeds in foreclosure but has distinct characteristics. This deed functions when a property is being sold to satisfy a judgment.

## What is a Sheriff's Deed?

A sheriff's deed is issued when a property is sold at a court-ordered public auction to satisfy a judgment. Anyone other than the judgment debtor who purchases this property receives a **Sheriff's Deed**, sometimes referred to as a **Referee's Deed**.

### Key Benefits of a Sheriff's Deed

-   The deed **clears away all unpaid junior liens** against the property.
-   This process helps the purchaser receive a **marketable title**, free of these encumbrances.

## Exception

-   If the **judgment debtor** purchases the property at the auction:
    -   The **junior liens are not removed** and remain attached to the property.

This nuance can affect the property's **title purity** and the new owner's responsibilities.

These details help navigate real estate transactions and make informed decisions.
