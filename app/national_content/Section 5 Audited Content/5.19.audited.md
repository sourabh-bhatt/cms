# 5.19 Further Assurances in Property Deeds

The concept of **Further Assurances** involves a promise from the person selling or transferring the property (the **grantor**) to the new owner (the **grantee**) to take any additional steps necessary in the future to ensure the **title** to the property is clear. This acts as protection. If something was overlooked or an error was made in the original deed, the grantor is obligated to help correct it.

## Purpose of Further Assurances

-   The grantor promises to execute any additional documents or take actions needed to fix title issues.
-   It protects the grantee by ensuring the property title is accurate and free of defects.

## Types of Documents Used for Further Assurances

-   **Correction Deed**: Used to rectify errors in a previously recorded deed, such as misspellings or incorrect property descriptions, ensuring all information is accurate.
-   **Quitclaim Deed**: Transfers any remaining interest the grantor might have in the property without making any guarantees about the title.

## The Covenant of Further Assurances

-   This covenant is not always included in deeds but serves as a form of protection when present.
-   If the grantor refuses to take necessary corrective actions or fails to cover related expenses, it is considered a breach of this covenant.
-   Further Assurances provides the new property owner with a means to address and rectify title issues that might have been missed initially.
