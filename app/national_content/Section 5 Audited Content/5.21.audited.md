# 5.21 Transferring Property Ownership: During Life and After Death

The concept of **transferring property ownership** includes both how it happens during an owner's lifetime and what occurs after their death. This affects **property rights** and ensures a smooth transition of ownership under various circumstances.

## Ownership Transfer During an Owner's Lifetime

Typically, when an owner is alive, they can transfer the **title** of their property through a **deed**.

## Ownership Transfer After Death

There are two primary ways ownership can transfer after an owner's death:

-   **Through a will**: The deceased owner's property is distributed according to their wishes as expressed in their will.
-   **Intestate succession**: If there is no will, ownership transfers according to state laws governing inheritance.

## Special Scenario: Joint Tenancy with Right of Survivorship

A unique situation arises when a property is co-owned by two or more people as **joint tenants with the right of survivorship**. In this case:

-   If one co-owner dies, the title of the property automatically passes to the surviving co-owner(s).
-   This transfer bypasses the will and intestate succession laws.
-   The deceased owner's share does not get tied up in legal proceedings or distributed to others through inheritance laws.

### Property Transfer Mechanisms

These mechanisms affect real estate professionals because:

-   They help in advising clients accurately about property ownership and transfer.
-   They clarify situations where ownership transfer after death may not follow the usual legal pathways.
-   They highlight the ease of property transfer between co-owners under joint tenancy with right of survivorship, avoiding complex legal processes.

This knowledge ensures smooth property transitions, regardless of the circumstances surrounding an owner's passing.
