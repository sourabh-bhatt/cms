# 5.17 Quitclaim Deeds

**Quitclaim deeds** differ from other types of deeds. The person transferring the property, known as the **grantor**, makes no promises regarding the property's **title status** with a quitclaim deed. The grantor passes the title *"as is,"* which means the property could come with **encumbrances** or issues attached to it.

## Key Characteristics of Quitclaim Deeds

-   The grantor does **not** promise to defend or warrant the quality of the title in any manner.
-   There is no assurance that the grantor has any interest or rights to convey.
-   The grantor essentially states, *"I'm not claiming any ownership here, but if I do have any rights to this property, now they're yours."*

## Common Uses of Quitclaim Deeds

Quitclaim deeds often come into play when:

-   There is a necessity to clear up any **defects on the title**.
-   The grantor needs to release any **claims against the title** they might have.

This deed addresses potential title issues without making any guarantees about the status of the title itself. These deeds handle situations where the ownership or claims against a property are not entirely clear.
