# 5.6 Components of Deeds in Real Estate

**Deeds** contain specific **clauses** for the transfer of **property ownership**. These legal documents include clauses that facilitate property transfers.

## Premises Clause

-   This is where the deed starts as it:
    -   Identifies the involved parties
    -   Describes the property being transferred
    -   Outlines any **consideration** (the purchase price or the exchange being made)

This section introduces all parties and property details.

## Habendum Clause

-   Specifies the type of **ownership** or interest being transferred.
-   Clarifies the rights the new owner will have.

Example: The property could be transferred in **fee simple**, which means the new owner has complete ownership and can do as they please with the property, subject to any laws or restrictions. The habendum defines the ownership interest being transferred.

## Testimonium Clause

-   This is where the deed gets officially sealed and delivered.
-   Often includes the date and the **grantor's** (the person transferring the property) signature.
-   Makes everything official, signaling that the grantor has indeed transferred the property rights to the **grantee** (the person receiving the property).

These clauses facilitate real estate transactions. They ensure that property transfers are conducted clearly, legally, and with a full understanding of the rights being transferred. Reading and interpreting these clauses ensures smooth property transactions.
