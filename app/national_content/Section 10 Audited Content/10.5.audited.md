# Understanding Cooperatives in Real Estate

<!-- When you're exploring different forms of property ownership, it's important to understand **cooperatives**, which are governed by state law and consist of individual units within a multi-unit real estate development. On the surface, cooperatives and condominiums might look similar, but the ownership structure between the two is quite different. -->
**Cooperatives** are governed by state law and consist of individual units within a multi-unit real estate development. Cooperatives and condominiums might look similar, but the ownership structure between the two is different.

## Ownership Structure of Cooperatives

- In a **cooperative**, the entire property - including all units and common areas - is owned by a **corporation, partnership, or land trust**.
- When purchasing a unit, buyers actually buy **stock in this corporation**, which is considered **personal property**.
- Along with the stock, buyers receive a **proprietary lease** for the specific unit, also personal property.
- This proprietary lease grants the right to occupy and use the unit for as long as the corporation exists.
- The term "proprietary" refers to ownership; as a tenant, there is an ownership stake through stock in the corporation.

## Transfer of Ownership and Financial Responsibilities

- When a unit is sold, both the **proprietary lease** and the seller's **stock** are transferred to the buyer.
- **Real estate taxes** and **mortgages** are tied to the entire cooperative property, not individual units.
- In case of foreclosure, the entire property is at risk, not just a single unit.

## Management of Cooperatives

- The cooperative is managed by the **corporation, partnership, or trust** that owns it.
- This entity operates under its own rules, which include:
  - Articles of incorporation
  - By-laws
  - Covenants and restrictions
  - House rules
- For corporations, a **board of directors** typically governs the cooperative and is responsible for setting unit prices and other management duties.

## Financial Obligations of Cooperative Residents

- There is no fixed rent in a cooperative.
- As a tenant and stockholder, residents pay a **monthly assessment**.
- This payment covers the share of the cooperative's operational costs, such as:
  - Taxes
  - Insurance
  - Mortgage payments
  - Management fees
  - Maintenance and repairs

## Differences Between Cooperatives and Condominiums

- In a cooperative:
  - Units are leased through a proprietary lease.
  - Stock is owned in the corporation that owns the entire development.

- In a condominium:
  - Individual units are owned **outright in fee simple**.
  - Common areas are owned as a **tenant-in-common**.
