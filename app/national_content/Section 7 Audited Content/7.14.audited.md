# Adjustable-Rate Mortgage (ARM)

An **Adjustable-Rate Mortgage**, or **ARM**, differs from **fixed-rate mortgages** because the interest rate can fluctuate. This fluctuation is tied to a specific **index**, which means the interest rate can move up or down.

## Adjustment Intervals

**Adjustment intervals** are predetermined periods after which the interest rate will be recalculated based on current market trends. Common intervals include:

- Every six months
- Annually

Example: A "one year ARM" means the interest rate adjustment happens once a year.

## Interest Rate Caps

The **interest rate cap** is a protective mechanism that limits how much the interest rate can change during each adjustment period or over the life of the loan. There are two types of caps:

- **Periodic caps** limit how much the rate can change at each adjustment (typically **2%**)
- **Lifetime caps** limit the total rate increase over the loan's life (typically **5% to 6%**)

These caps keep changes within a manageable range, preventing sudden increases in the interest rate.

## Risks and Benefits of ARMs

ARMs have risks and benefits:

- They offer **flexibility** and potential **savings** for borrowers under the right market conditions.
- They carry the risk of future payment increases if interest rates rise.

ARMs provide different financing options compared to fixed-rate mortgages.
