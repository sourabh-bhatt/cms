# Understanding Junior Mortgages in Real Estate

**Junior mortgages** function within mortgage and real estate frameworks. Multiple loans on single properties create priority orders for payment when borrowers default.

## What is a Junior Mortgage?

A **junior mortgage** is any loan secured against a property that ranks below the first or primary mortgage in terms of priority.

- If the borrower defaults (cannot keep up with loan payments), the lender of the **first mortgage** has the first claim on the property's value.
- This claim is used to recover the outstanding debt.
- Only after the first mortgage is fully satisfied can the lenders of the junior mortgages claim any remaining value.

## Risks and Interest Rates

Because junior mortgages are second in line (or third, fourth, etc.) to be paid, they come with increased risk for lenders. To compensate for this risk, junior mortgages usually have **higher interest rates**.

## Example of a Junior Mortgage

A common example of a junior mortgage is a **home equity loan**.

## Mortgage Hierarchy Applications

Loan priority hierarchy affects risk management in real estate financing and lending processes.
