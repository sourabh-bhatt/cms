# Defeasance Clause in Mortgages and Deeds of Trust

A **defeasance clause** in **mortgages** and **deeds of trust** defines the process for "defeating" or canceling the mortgage or deed of trust once the debt is repaid in full.

When the borrower pays off the entire loan, this clause ensures that the loan is considered null and void, and the borrower is restored to full rights of **property ownership**.

## Steps After Loan Repayment

After the defeasance clause takes effect, an additional step must be completed:

- The **lien** must be removed from the **public record**.
- This is done by recording a **satisfaction of mortgage** or a **deed of release**.

Without this step, the lien, although practically ineffective, remains part of the public record, potentially complicating future transactions involving the property.

Following these procedures ensures a smooth transition back to full ownership rights.
