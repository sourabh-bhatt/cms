# Buying Property "Subject To" an Existing Mortgage

Buying property **"subject to"** an existing mortgage differs from mortgage assumption. This approach involves purchasing properties with existing mortgages without taking personal responsibility for the debt.

## How Buying "Subject To" Works

- Buyers purchasing property **"subject to"** do **not take personal responsibility** for existing mortgage debt. This affects financial liability in adverse situations.

- Example: If mortgage payments cease and the property enters **foreclosure**, you as the **"subject to" buyer** may lose the property. This represents an inherent purchase risk.

- However, **buyers face no personal liability for remaining debt**. If foreclosure sales fail to cover full mortgage debt, lenders cannot pursue buyers for shortfalls.

## Lender's Recourse and Financial Protection

- The lender's recourse is limited to the **original mortgagor** or seller of the property. They are the parties who could potentially be sued for any shortfall.

- This limitation provides a layer of **financial protection** for buyers purchasing a property **"subject to"** the existing mortgage debt.

This concept affects real estate purchase decisions and risk assessment strategies.
