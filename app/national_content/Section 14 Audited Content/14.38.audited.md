# Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)

<!-- When you enter the world of real estate transactions, you'll encounter a critical piece of legislation known as the **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)**. -->
The **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)** is federal legislation in real estate transactions. This federal law, overseen by the **Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)**, regulates **closing procedures**. <!-- plays a significant role -->Its primary function is to ensure that both buyers and sellers are fully aware of all **settlement costs** while also protecting them from unfair practices by lenders.

**Key Points about RESPA:**

- RESPA does *not* dictate the prices for settlement services.
- It establishes rules regarding who can act as a **settlement agent** and their responsibilities.
- These measures aim to reduce confusion and provide transparency about the costs and processes involved in closing a real estate deal.

## Scope of RESPA

RESPA applies to a range of loans secured by mortgages on residential properties with one to four family units. This includes:

- Purchase loans
- Refinances
- Property improvement loans
- Lines of credit

However, certain transactions fall outside the scope of RESPA, such as:

- Business purpose loans
- Loans for purchasing vacant land

## Required Disclosures Under RESPA

<!-- A cornerstone of RESPA is the requirement for lenders to provide key disclosures at various stages of the loan application and settlement process. -->
RESPA requires lenders to provide disclosures at various stages of the loan application and settlement process.

### Initial Disclosures (Within Three Business Days of Loan Application)

Lenders must provide the borrower with:

- **Special Information Booklet**
- **Homeownership Counseling References**
- **Loan Estimate**
- **Mortgage Servicing Statement**

<!-- These documents equip the borrower with essential information about the mortgage process, including an estimate of the settlement charges they are likely to face. -->
These documents provide borrowers with information about the mortgage process, including an estimate of the settlement charges they are likely to face.

### Affiliations Disclosure

RESPA mandates disclosure of any affiliations between businesses providing settlement services. This ensures borrowers are aware they have the freedom to choose their service providers, although certain exceptions exist.

### Closing Disclosure

At least three days prior to settlement, lenders must provide a **Closing Disclosure** that details the actual fees to be paid, offering a clear picture of the financial obligations at closing. If there are changes to the loan terms after this disclosure, a new three-day review period may be required.

## Escrow Account Regulations

RESPA addresses the handling of **escrow accounts**, setting limitations on the amounts lenders can require borrowers to deposit for future charges related to the property, such as:

- Taxes
- Insurance

These provisions ensure that borrowers are not overcharged.

## Prohibitions Under RESPA

<!-- Understanding the prohibitions under RESPA is equally crucial. -->
The act forbids:

- **Kickbacks**
- **Referral fees**
- Charging of **unearned fees**

These prohibitions apply to settlement service business involving federally related mortgage loans. Violations can result in severe penalties, including criminal charges or hefty fines in a civil lawsuit.
