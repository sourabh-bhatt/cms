# Understanding Loan-to-Value Ratios (LTV)

The **Loan-to-Value (LTV) ratio** is a cornerstone concept in real estate financing. It compares the amount of the mortgage loan to the lower of the sales price or the appraised value of the property. Lenders use the LTV ratio as a critical factor to gauge the risk level when evaluating a loan application.

## What the LTV Ratio Indicates

- A **higher LTV ratio** means a smaller down payment because the lender is covering a larger portion of the purchase price.
<!-- - A **lower LTV ratio** indicates a larger down payment, meaning you are borrowing less of the purchase price. -->
- A **lower LTV ratio** indicates a larger down payment, meaning the borrower is borrowing less of the purchase price.

Lenders consider a high LTV ratio riskier because the borrower has less equity in the property. More equity, which comes from a larger down payment, reduces the likelihood of loan default.

## Calculating the LTV Ratio

The basic formula to calculate the LTV ratio is:


LTV = Loan Amount ÷ Property Value


Alternatively, to find the loan amount based on a known LTV ratio:


Loan Amount = Property Value × LTV


### Example 1: Calculating LTV Ratio

- Property value: $80,000
- Loan amount: $60,000

Calculation:


LTV = $60,000 ÷ $80,000 = 0.75


Expressed as a percentage, the LTV ratio is **75%**.

### Example 2: Calculating Loan Amount

- Property value: $80,000
- LTV ratio: 75%

Calculation:


Loan Amount = $80,000 × 0.75 = $60,000
