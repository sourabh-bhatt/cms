# Truth in Lending Act (TILA) and Regulation Z in Real Estate Financing

<!-- When learning about real estate financing, it's essential to grasp the significant role played by the **Truth in Lending Act (TILA)** and **Regulation Z**. -->
The **Truth in Lending Act (TILA)** and **Regulation Z** play a role in real estate financing. These federal laws require clear disclosure of all terms related to consumer credit and leases. <!-- This helps you make better decisions by comparing various financial options. -->
This helps consumers make better decisions by comparing various financial options.

## Background and Purpose of TILA

- Enacted in 1968 as part of the Consumer Credit Protection Act and enforced through Regulation Z.
- Fixed the problem of ads showing only attractive terms while hiding the real costs.
- Requires anyone promoting consumer credit or leases - including real estate brokers and builders, not just creditors and lessors - to provide comprehensive information to consumers.
- Media hosting advertisements are not held accountable under TILA.

## Concepts Under TILA and Regulation Z

### Loan Comparisons and APR

- Focuses on the total borrowing cost or **Annual Percentage Rate (APR)**, which includes both the interest rate and any associated fees.
- Before TILA, ads could mislead people by showing low monthly payments without sharing details such as down payments, total number of payments, or the APR.

### Scope of Application

- Applies to many types of credit:
  - **Consumer loans** like credit cards (**open-end credit**).
  - Loans secured by real estate (**closed-end credit**).
  - Personal property leases.

- **Consumer credit** includes both closed-end and open-end credit used primarily for personal, family, or household purposes.
- A **consumer lease** refers to personal property leases longer than four months for similar personal uses.
- Does not apply to business loans, farm loans, or loans over certain dollar amounts.

## Documents Under TILA

- **Loan Estimate:** Provided to borrowers early in the loan process, outlining anticipated fees.
- **Closing Disclosure:** Provided at closing, detailing actual fees.
- These documents are not required for certain loans, such as home equity lines of credit or mobile home loans, which instead require simplified initial and final TILA statements.

## Additional Protections and Requirements

- **Homeownership Counseling:** For first-time borrowers before finalizing loans with potential negative amortization.

- **Adjustable-Rate Mortgages (ARMs):** Lenders must provide detailed information about the variability of interest rates.

- **Right of Rescission:** Borrowers have the right to withdraw from a mortgage agreement within three business days after closing, receipt of the notice, or all material disclosures - whichever occurs last.

## Advertising and Trigger Terms

- Specific **trigger terms** in advertisements (e.g., down payment amounts, finance charges, number of payments, repayment period) require comprehensive TILA disclosures.
- If any trigger term is used, the advertisement must also disclose other loan features, such as the APR.

- Violations of these advertising standards can lead to enforcement actions including injunctions and civil penalties.
