# Real Estate Appraisals and Federal Regulations
<!-- When you're diving into the world of real estate financing, one key component you need to understand is the **importance and requirement of appraisals**. -->
Real estate financing involves a key component: the **requirement of appraisals**.

## Appraisal Requirements

Most lenders, whether offering **conventional loans** or **government-backed programs**, require an appraisal to assess the value of the property used as collateral for a mortgage. This requirement is backed by **Federal law**.

The **Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989 (FIRREA)** regulates the appraisal profession. It mandates the inclusion of appraisals in certain real estate transactions to ensure fair and accurate valuation of properties.

## Licensing and Certification of Appraisers

- **Appraiser licenses** are issued by individual states, not the federal government.
- There is a key distinction between being **licensed** and **certified**:
  - A **licensed appraiser** is authorized to perform appraisals.
  - A **certified appraiser** has met additional minimum federal standards set by FIRREA, including:
    - Education requirements
    - Examination scores
    - Experience in the field

## When Are Appraisals Required?

FIRREA, along with other federal laws from agencies such as the **Federal Housing Administration (FHA)** and the **Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)**, establishes when appraisals are mandatory. Key points include:

- Certain **federally related loans** require appraisals, specifically for transactions of $250,000 or more.
- Appraisals must be conducted by either **licensed** or **certified appraisers**.
- The requirement depends on the **property's value** and the **specific nature of the loan**.
