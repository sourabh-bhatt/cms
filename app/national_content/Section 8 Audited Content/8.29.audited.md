# Estate at Sufferance in Leaseholds

An **Estate at Sufferance** relates to how **leaseholds** operate, especially when **legal occupancy** periods come into play.

## What is an Estate at Sufferance?

An estate at sufferance occurs under very specific conditions—it's what happens when someone continues to occupy a property after their **legal rights** to do so have expired. This could be a **tenant** whose **lease has ended** but hasn't moved out yet.

- This type of estate carries the **minimum rights possible**, putting it at the bottom of the hierarchy in terms of **property estates**.
- Since it exists due to the occupant overstaying their welcome, it's heavily encumbered with limitations.

## Duration and Termination

The continuation of an estate at sufferance is rather precarious; it lasts either until:

1. The occupant decides to leave, or
2. The **property owner** takes action to remove them.

The landlord may terminate this estate without advance notice, since technically, the tenant's stay is no longer lawful. However, proper legal procedures must still be followed for removal.

## Common Scenarios

A common scenario involving an estate at sufferance is with a **holdover tenant**. This is someone who hasn't vacated the property when required, effectively overstaying their lease.

- This situation can also occur with a seller or renter who, despite agreements or contracts that stipulate a move-out date, fails to leave.
- Such failure unintentionally creates an estate at sufferance.

## Estate at Sufferance in Real Estate

This concept highlights:

- **Clear, enforceable agreements** in property transactions.
- How disputes over **occupancy rights** are handled in real estate.
