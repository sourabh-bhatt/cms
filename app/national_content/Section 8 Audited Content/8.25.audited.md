# Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act: Key Concepts for Residential Leases

The **Virginia Residential Landlord and Tenant Act** impacts all residential rental agreements, whether written or verbal. This includes properties ranging from single-family to multi-family dwellings. The Act covers leases within public housing under **HUD regulation**, though HUD's own rules take precedence where there's a discrepancy.

## Applicability and Exceptions

The Act applies broadly but excludes certain situations, including:

- Commercial spaces
- Prisons or schools
- Fraternity houses
- Campgrounds
- Situations where the tenant isn't paying rent
- Employees of the landlord living on-site for work reasons
- Transient housing such as hotels if staying less than 90 days without a signed lease
- Landlords who rent out a maximum of two single-family homes, provided there's a specific statement in the lease allowing them to opt out

In these cases, the general landlord and tenant laws apply instead.

## Application Fees and Deposits

- Landlords can request a **refundable deposit** when tenants apply, in addition to an application fee of up to $50.
- If the landlord decides not to rent to applicants, they must return the deposit within 20 days.

## Security Deposits

- Maximum security deposit allowed is **two months' rent**.
- Deposits must be kept in a **separate account**.
- Upon lease termination, any remaining deposit after deductions for damages or unpaid rent must be returned within **45 days**.
- If deductions are made, the landlord must provide a **detailed list** of charges.
- Tenants have the right to be present during the **final walkthrough**, which should occur within **72 hours after the lease ends**.

## Lease Violations and Notices

- **Non-payment of rent:** Landlord can issue a **5-day notice** to pay or vacate before starting eviction proceedings.
- **Bounced checks:** Tenant may be required to pay in cash or money order for future rent payments.
- **Other lease breaches:** Landlord must provide at least **30 days** written notice to correct the issue.
- For **urgent health or safety threats**, landlords may enter the property to address the problem and charge the tenant for the work.

## Landlord Responsibilities and Tenant Remedies

- If the landlord fails to uphold their obligations, the tenant must notify them **in writing** and allow **30 days** to fix the issue (shorter in emergencies).
- If unresolved, tenants may pay rent to the **local court** until the problem is corrected.
- Tenants cannot break the lease without a judge's approval.

## Landlord Rights and Rules

- Setting **house rules**
- Entering the property with proper notice
- Handling lease renewals and **abandoned property**
- Safeguarding tenant's personal information, with some exceptions
- Informing tenants if the property is being **foreclosed** on

## Tenant Rights

- Installing **security devices**
- Requesting permission to **sublease**
- Protection against unnecessary landlord intrusions
- Breaking the lease under specific circumstances, such as:
  - **Military deployment**
  - Being a **victim of abuse**

  (Proper notice and documentation are required.)

These laws and responsibilities affect navigation of residential lease complexities.
