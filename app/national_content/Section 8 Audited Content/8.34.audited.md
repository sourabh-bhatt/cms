# Estate for Years Lease

The **Estate for Years**, also known as a **tenancy for years**, is a type of lease agreement with a definitive start and end date, distinguishing it from leases that automatically renew.

## Key Characteristics of an Estate for Years

- The term "years" does *not* strictly mean the lease must last for years; it can be any specific duration, ranging from a single day or week to a decade or more.
- The lease ends automatically on the expiration date without requiring notice from either the **lessor** (the owner) or the **lessee** (the tenant).

## Stability of the Estate for Years Lease

- The lease is considered **personal property** to the lessee, meaning it remains valid even if the property is sold or if the owner dies.
- The tenant retains the right to remain in possession of the leased property under the original lease terms despite changes in ownership.

## Comparison with Other Lease Types

- Unlike an **estate at will** or an **estate at sufferance**, which lack stability and predictability, the Estate for Years provides clear rights and obligations for both parties.

These distinctions influence how leases are managed and the rights of all parties involved. Estate for Years affects lease agreement navigation and negotiation, ensuring that both landlords and tenants are aware of their rights and obligations from the outset.
