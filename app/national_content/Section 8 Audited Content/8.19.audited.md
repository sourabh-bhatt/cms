# Leases in Real Estate

**Leasing** is the process where the right of exclusive use and possession of a real estate property is transferred. This transfer occurs from the **landlord** (or **lessor**) to the tenant (the **lessee**). 

Through a lease, tenants gain the rights to use the property but do *not* gain ownership. Because ownership is not transferred, the lease is recognized as **personal property**, not **real property**.

## Types of Lease Agreements

- Leases can be established through **written** or **oral agreements**.
- **Oral contracts** are only valid if the lease period is **less than one year**.
- Most leases are:
  - **Bilateral agreements** (two-way agreements)
  - **Executory contracts** (not yet fully performed or fulfilled)
  - Sometimes **assignable**, meaning the rights under the lease can be transferred to another party.
- Lease durations vary and can be for a **fixed period** or **undefined**, depending on the tenancy type.

## Validity of a Lease Contract

For a lease to be legally valid, it must meet the fundamental **elements of a contract**:

1. **Offer and acceptance**
2. **Consideration** (something of value exchanged)
3. No existing **legal defenses** that could invalidate the contract

The main parties involved are:

- The **lessor** (property owner or landlord)
- The **lessee** (tenant)

- The person being sublet to is the **sublessee**.
- The sublessee pays rent to the original tenant, not to the landlord.
- The sublessee has no contractual relationship with the landlord.
- Many leases include clauses that **limit or prohibit subletting** without the landlord's prior approval.

## Assignment of Lease

The **assignment of a lease** occurs when a tenant transfers *all* rights and interests in the leased property to another party, called the **assignee**.

- After assignment, the assignee generally becomes primarily responsible to the landlord.
- The original lessee (or **assignor**) may still carry **secondary liability**.

These components and dynamics affect lease navigation and ensure that terms serve the interests and responsibilities of all parties involved.
