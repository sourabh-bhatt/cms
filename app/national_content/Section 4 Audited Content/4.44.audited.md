# Option Contracts in Real Estate

Option contracts must include three essential elements, just like any other contract. These are:

- **Offer and acceptance**
- **Consideration** (which often means money or value in exchange)
- **Legal capacity** of the parties

## Requirements for a Valid Option Contract

<!-- In addition to the basic contract elements, an option contract in the real estate context must specify several important details: -->

An option contract in the real estate context must specify several details:

- **Clear Price**: The contract must state a clear price for the property. This price is *non-negotiable* once the option contract is in place. The holder has the exclusive right to purchase or lease the property at that price within a specified time frame.

- **Expiration Date**: The option contract must clearly outline the expiration date of the option. This is the deadline by which the holder must decide whether to exercise the option to buy or lease the property. Missing this deadline means losing the right, and the property owner can then offer the property to others.

- **Property Description**: A thorough description of the property must be included. This can be either:
  - A **legal description**, which is often detailed and technical, reflecting how the property is described in legal records
  - A **non-legal description**, which might be less technical but should still accurately convey what the property entails

## Exercising the Option

If the holder decides to exercise the option, the option contract essentially transforms into a **purchase agreement** or **lease**, depending on the terms. This indicates moving forward to acquire or lease the property under the terms initially agreed upon in the option contract.
