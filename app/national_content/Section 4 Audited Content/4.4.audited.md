# Understanding Unilateral and Bilateral Contracts

<!-- When you're diving into the world of **contracts**, it's crucial to understand the difference between **unilateral** and **bilateral contracts** because this distinction determines how many parties are obligated to perform a task. -->

## Unilateral Contracts

In a **unilateral contract**, only *one party* is obligated to take action. Think of it as a *one-way promise*. 

- **Example:** Someone offers a reward for a lost pet. The person promising the reward doesn't expect the finder to promise to search for the pet; instead, they're only obligated to pay the reward if someone returns the pet.

In this type of contract, a single party commits to perform a task, but that commitment is triggered by another's action, *not* their promise.

## Bilateral Contracts

On the flip side, **bilateral contracts** are more of a *two-way street*. In these contracts, *both parties* make promises and are obligated to fulfill them.

- **Example:** Buying a house. The buyer promises to pay a certain amount, and the seller promises to hand over the title to the property. Each party has a clear obligation to the other, established from the start.

## Understanding Contract Types

<!-- Understanding whether a contract is unilateral or bilateral helps you grasp: -->
Understanding whether a contract is unilateral or bilateral helps determine:

- Who is required to do what
- Under what conditions those obligations apply
