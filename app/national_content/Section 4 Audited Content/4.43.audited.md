# The Mailbox Rule in Contract Acceptance

## The Mailbox Rule

<!-- In learning about contracts, it's crucial to understand the concept of the **Mailbox Rule**. This rule is particularly significant when you're dealing with **acceptances made via mail**. Here's how it works:

- Suppose you decide to accept an offer by mailing your acceptance. -->

The **Mailbox Rule** applies to **acceptances made via mail**. Here's how it works:

- When accepting an offer by mailing the acceptance.
- According to the **Mailbox Rule**, the acceptance becomes effective the moment it is deposited into the mailbox.

<!-- This is a critical point —it's *not* when your acceptance physically reaches the offeror, but the moment it's placed in the mailbox that matters. -->

The acceptance is effective when placed in the mailbox, not when it physically reaches the offeror.

## Differentiating Acceptance Methods

<!-- It's important to differentiate between acceptances made through mail and other forms of acceptance: -->

Acceptances made through mail differ from other forms of acceptance:

- **Mail acceptances**: Effective upon mailing (deposit into mailbox).
- **Other methods** (email, phone call, direct communication): Only become binding when actually received by the offeror.
