# 4.73 Promissory Estoppel in Contract Law

**Promissory estoppel** serves as a legal principle in contract law.

## Definition and Application

Example: One party makes a promise to another party. The recipient takes action based on that promise and makes decisions they would not have made otherwise. Later, the promising party attempts to withdraw, claiming the agreement was not a real "contract" because some elements were missing. **Promissory estoppel** applies in such situations.

Promissory estoppel is a legal safeguard. It upholds fairness in the legal system. This is called *Equity*. It stops someone from going back on their word under certain conditions. This happens when backing out would be unfair to the other party.

## Criteria for Invoking Promissory Estoppel

For promissory estoppel to apply, three things must happen:

1.  **Clear Promise:** One party made a promise. They expected the other party to rely on this promise.
2.  **Reasonable Reliance:** The other party did rely on this promise. They changed their actions or decisions. They believed the promise would be kept.
3.  **Detriment or Harm:** Relying on the promise caused harm. This happened when the promising party tried to go back on their word.

## Contract Law Applications

Contracts are not always perfect. Sometimes written agreements are incomplete or certain formalities were missed. When both parties acted as if a valid contract existed, **promissory estoppel** could determine the outcome of disputes.

## Practical Considerations

-   Courts can be unpredictable when applying promissory estoppel.
-   Courts have broad discretion to decide when fairness requires its use.

Clear and formal agreements help avoid reliance on promissory estoppel doctrines.
