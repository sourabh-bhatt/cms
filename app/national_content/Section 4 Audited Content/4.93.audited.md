# 4.93 Understanding Reliance in Real Estate Contracts

The concept of **reliance** applies to **real estate contracts**.

## The Concept of Reliance in Real Estate

Example: A seller promises to complete certain repairs on a property before closing. Trusting this promise, a buyer proceeds with the purchase and turns down other properties, expecting those repairs to be completed.

## Consideration in Real Estate Contracts

At first glance, it might seem like there is no **consideration** in this setup. *Consideration* refers to the idea that both parties in a contract agree to give or do something of value. In this scenario, it might appear that only the seller is committing to something, leaving the agreement somewhat lopsided. This situation prompts a discussion about the very nature and necessity of consideration in forming contracts.

## Court Enforcement Despite Lack of Consideration

However, despite the lack of clear consideration, courts can step in and enforce such an agreement to act as a contract. Why?

-   To avoid an unfair outcome
-   Because the buyer's decision to proceed with the purchase, based on the promise made, placed the buyer in a vulnerable position

By choosing to enforce the agreement, courts aim to rectify the imbalance created by the buyer's reliance on the promise, thereby protecting the buyer's interests in the real estate transaction.

These nuances of **contract law** apply in real estate, particularly when recognizing how legal principles can offer protection in situations where agreements might not seem enforceable at first glance.
