# Impossibility in Real Estate Contracts

<!-- Let's consider a scenario where you and another party have agreed to close on a house within a specified time frame. Now, imagine that before the closing date, the house is unfortunately destroyed by fire. Despite having a valid **sales contract**, you would no longer be obligated to complete the closing. -->

Example: Consider a scenario where two parties have agreed to close on a house within a specified time frame. If the house is destroyed by fire before the closing date, the buyer would no longer be obligated to complete the closing despite having a valid **sales contract**.

This is because it has become *impossible* for the seller to fulfill their end of the bargain—namely, to deliver the house as agreed. In legal terms, this situation exemplifies the concept of **impossibility**, where unforeseen events render the performance of the contract unachievable.
