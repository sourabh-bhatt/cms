# Mutual Mistake in Real Estate Contracts

<!-- Imagine you're entering into a contract with someone else, and both of you think you're talking about different things without realizing it. For instance, consider this example: -->

Example: Two parties enter into a contract, and both think they're talking about different things without realizing it:

- The buyer thinks they're agreeing to buy one particular lot in a new subdivision.
- The other party, the seller, believes they're selling the buyer a different lot.

<!-- This misunderstanding isn't because someone was trying to deceive the other; both of you honestly made a mistake. -->
This misunderstanding isn't because someone was trying to deceive the other; both parties honestly made a mistake. If this situation isn't resolved mutually, the contract can be considered *voidable*. This occurs because there wasn't a **"meeting of the minds,"** a fundamental aspect of contract validity.

## Key Points About Mutual Mistake

- The mistake was *mutual*: both parties misunderstood which property was in question.
- The mistake was *unintentional*: neither party intended to make the mistake.
- The mistake was *material*: the specific property being transacted is central to the contract, given every piece of property is unique.

<!-- Understanding this concept is crucial because it underscores the importance of clear communication and precise agreements in contracts, especially in real estate where each property is distinct. -->
This concept affects clear communication and precise agreements in contracts, especially in real estate where each property is distinct.
