# 4.92 Calculating Monetary Damages in a Breach of Contract

Example: A **builder** enters into a contract to construct a home for **$100,000**. Before construction begins, the buyer decides to back out of the deal, and there is no clause in the contract that discusses a set amount for damages, known as **liquidated damages**.

## How to Calculate Damages Without a Liquidated Damages Clause

In such a situation, calculating monetary claims for this **breach of contract** involves the following:

-   The project would have cost **$90,000** to complete. These are the **expenses** required to build the home.
-   **Monetary damages** are not the entire $100,000 contract amount.
-   Instead, damages are calculated based on the **profit** missed due to the buyer's default.

Therefore, actual monetary damages would be **$10,000**. This figure represents the **profit margin** that would have been earned if the house had been built as planned.

## Contract Value and Profit Distinction

This concept requires distinguishing between:

-   The **total contract value** (the $100,000 agreed upon)
-   The **actual profit** expected from the project (the $10,000 profit margin)

This differentiation ensures that claims for damages are fair and reflect the true **financial loss** incurred due to the breach.
