# Conditions and Contingencies in Contracts

<!-- In mastering the art of **contracts**, you'll encounter something called **conditions** or **contingencies**. **Conditions** or **contingencies** are specific events or milestones that must occur before any party is obligated to fulfill their end of the contract. These conditions are often flagged by keywords like *"if"*, *"when"*, *"unless"*, or *"provided"*.

## Conditions

- Conditions act as **gatekeepers** rather than direct commitments.
- If a condition fails to materialize, neither party is typically liable for damages.
- They set the stage for **contract obligations** but do not enforce them directly.

## Role and Impact of Conditions

- A condition can be any event and may be the responsibility of either party to fulfill.
- Conditions enhance the ability to draft and negotiate contracts effectively.
- Proper navigation of conditions ensures all milestones are met, facilitating smooth transactions.

Example: If a contract states, "*The buyer will purchase the property **if** the home inspection is satisfactory,*" the buyer is not obligated to complete the purchase unless the inspection condition is met.
