# 4.96 Understanding Contingencies in Real Estate Purchase Transactions

**Contingencies** are specific provisions or conditions that need to be fulfilled during the **executory period** of the contract, which is the time between the contract's signing and its closing, before parties are obligated to complete the transaction. Contingencies are signaled by terms like *"if"*, *"when"*, *"unless"*, or *"provided"*.

## Legal Framework for Contingencies

If a contingency isn't met, the contract can become **voidable** at the discretion of the party that the contingency is intended to protect or benefit. This provides a safety net to both buyers and sellers, ensuring that they are not forced into a deal that doesn't meet the predefined conditions.

## Examples of Common Contingencies

-   **Finance Contingency**: The buyer is not bound to proceed with the purchase if unable to secure a loan from a lender.
-   **Home Inspection Contingency**: Allows the buyer to withdraw from the deal if an inspection reveals significant issues with the property.

Effective use and negotiation of contingencies ensures that transactions are both fair and aligned with party needs.
