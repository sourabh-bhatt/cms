# Understanding Unilateral Contracts in Real Estate

<!-- Let's explore the notion of a **unilateral contract**, especially as seen in **option contracts**, to deepen your understanding. -->
A **unilateral contract**, especially as seen in **option contracts**, operates differently from bilateral agreements.

## What is a Unilateral Contract?

In a unilateral contract, one party makes a **promise** or an **offer** that becomes binding only when the other party performs a specific act. This form of contract presents a **one-sided obligation**. This means:

- Only one party makes a promise or commitment.
- The contract becomes binding only when the other party completes the requested action.
- There is no mutual exchange of promises.

## How Does It Work?

<!-- To break it down, think of it as a scenario where: -->
In this type of contract:

- Someone makes a promise in exchange for a specific action.
- The contract becomes binding only when the other party performs that action.
- Acceptance happens through performance, not through words or signatures.
- Once performance begins, the offeror cannot revoke the offer.

## Real Estate Example: Option Contracts

An **option contract** is a common unilateral contract in real estate:

- A property owner promises to sell their property at a specific price.
- The buyer pays an option fee for the right to purchase.
- The buyer can choose to exercise the option or let it expire.
- The seller must sell if the buyer exercises the option.
- The buyer has no obligation to purchase.
