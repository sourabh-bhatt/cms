# Impact of Supply and Demand on Real Estate Prices

## Basic Concept of Supply and Demand

- When a product is highly sought after but in scarce supply, its price tends to increase because the market can support a higher price tag.
- Conversely, if there's an abundance of the product but little interest, its price is likely to drop due to the surplus.

## Unique Characteristics of Real Estate

Applying supply and demand to real estate reveals some complexities because real estate does not balance as straightforwardly as manufactured goods do. Constructing new buildings isn't instantaneous, and oftentimes, there's limited space for new development. It is also impossible to relocate existing structures to areas with higher demand. These factors introduce distinctive characteristics of real estate:

1. **Heterogeneity**
   - Every real estate piece is one of a kind, distinguished by its location and the quality of any structures on it.
   - Unlike mass-produced items, real estate cannot remedy a supply shortage simply by creating identical units.
   - Lands are unique and finite. While you might develop more living spaces vertically, such as in condominiums, the land's area remains unchangeable.

2. **Immobility**
   - Real estate is stationary, meaning buyers must come to the property.
   - This immobility causes market dynamics to differ significantly from those of movable goods.
   - Real estate is always subject to the local market's ebbs and flows, making geographic economic conditions crucial.

## Effects of Supply and Demand on Real Estate Prices

Because of these characteristics, the effects of supply and demand manifest uniquely:

- *Over-supply leading to lower prices*: When there is an excess of homes or land for sale, prices typically dip. The inability to redistribute properties to balance discrepancies keeps prices variable and heavily dependent on local supply.

- *Under-supply leading to higher prices*: Scarcity in available land or homes tends to drive prices up. Even though more construction is possible, delays inherent in building cannot meet immediate demand surges, leading to price hikes.
