# Understanding the Closing Disclosure in Real Estate Transactions

<!-- When buying a home, you need to understand the **Closing Disclosure**, an important document required by two federal laws: the **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)** and the **Truth in Lending Act (TILA)**. These laws require that the lender give you a Closing Disclosure at least **three business days before the transaction's settlement**. The **Closing Disclosure** is a document required by two federal laws: the **Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act (RESPA)** and the **Truth in Lending Act (TILA)**. These laws require that lenders provide borrowers a Closing Disclosure at least **three business days before the transaction's settlement**. This form shows the services provided and the actual fees charged in the transaction.

## Key Aspects of the Closing Disclosure

- **Timing:** The Closing Disclosure must be provided at least three business days prior to settlement.
- **Content:** It details the services involved and the exact fees charged.

## Changes After Delivery and Their Implications

<!-- You need to know what happens if changes are made after you get the Closing Disclosure. If there are any changes to the main loan terms, such as: -->

Changes made after borrowers receive the Closing Disclosure have specific implications. If there are any changes to the main loan terms, such as:

- An increase in the **Annual Percentage Rate (APR)**
- The introduction of a new **prepayment penalty**
- A switch to a different kind of loan

<!-- these changes will create a **new three-day waiting period before closing**. This rule makes sure you understand all changes before finalizing the deal. -->

these changes create a **new three-day waiting period before closing**. This rule ensures borrowers understand all changes before finalizing the deal.

## Role of the Settlement Agent

<!-- Lenders can authorize the **settlement agent** to deliver the Closing Disclosure directly to you, the borrower, on their behalf. Additionally, the settlement agent might complete some or all of the information required on this form. This helps make the process easier and ensures you get the right information. -->

Lenders can authorize the **settlement agent** to deliver the Closing Disclosure directly to borrowers on their behalf. Additionally, the settlement agent might complete some or all of the information required on this form. This makes the process easier and ensures borrowers receive accurate information.

## Closing Disclosure Requirements for the Seller

The seller must also receive a Closing Disclosure, which can be provided on or before the settlement date. This requirement can be fulfilled by:

- Providing the seller with a copy of the buyer's Closing Disclosure if it includes information relevant to the seller's side of the deal
- Providing a separate disclosure that contains only seller-specific information
