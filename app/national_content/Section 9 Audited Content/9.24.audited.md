# Covenant to Pay Taxes in Real Estate

<!-- When you're dealing with properties, one key concept you need to be familiar with is the **_Covenant to Pay Taxes_**. -->
The **_Covenant to Pay Taxes_** is a promise made by the **borrower** to handle all **real estate taxes** and other **assessments** that might be imposed on the property.

## Lender's Perspective on Tax Covenants

<!-- Understanding this covenant matters greatly, especially for lenders, because of the **hierarchy of claims** on the property: -->
This covenant affects lenders due to the **hierarchy of claims** on the property:

- If the borrower fails to pay the real estate taxes, these unpaid taxes become a **lien** on the property.
- This lien is a **superior claim**, meaning it takes precedence over other claims, including those of the lender.

## Foreclosure and Distribution of Sale Proceeds

<!-- Imagine a scenario where the property must be sold due to **foreclosure**. -->
Example: When property must be sold due to **foreclosure**, the money obtained from the sale is distributed in a specific order:

1. First, any outstanding **real estate taxes** are paid off.
2. Only after taxes are cleared does the lender receive funds toward repaying the **principal amount** owed.

This order of payment highlights why lenders closely monitor whether taxes are paid. It directly impacts their ability to **recover the loaned amount** if the borrower defaults.
