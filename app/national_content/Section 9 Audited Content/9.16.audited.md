# Understanding General and Specific Liens

<!-- When you're diving into the world of **liens**, it's crucial to grasp the distinction between **general** and **specific liens**, as this knowledge will significantly enhance your understanding and capabilities in handling various property-related situations. -->
The distinction between **general** and **specific liens** affects handling various property-related situations.

## General Liens

General liens apply broadly to all of a debtor's assets, including both **personal** and **real property**. Example: If someone owes a large amount of money to the government or has been involved in a legal judgment, a general lien could be placed on everything they own - from a car to a house. Common examples of general liens include:

- **Federal tax liens**
- Certain **judicial liens** stemming from court judgments

## Specific Liens

Specific liens are more targeted and attach only to particular pieces of property. Examples include:

- A **mortgage lien** on a house, which is linked explicitly to that property
- Unpaid **property taxes**
- A **mechanic's lien** when a contractor has not been paid for work done on a property

With specific liens, only the property specified under the lien can be taken to settle the debt.

- Assessment of situations with precision
- Advisory on courses of action
- Identification of potential risks and opportunities when dealing with property
