# Understanding Liens in Real Estate

<!-- When you're diving into the realm of real estate, understanding **liens** is crucial. Think of a lien as a *legal claim* against a property. -->
A lien is a *legal claim* against a property. This claim is used as security for the repayment of a debt, meaning that a property can be used as **collateral**.

## What is a Lien?

- A lien is a legal claim against a property.
- It serves as security for repayment of a debt.
- The property acts as collateral for the debt.

<!-- If someone who has a loan secured by a lien fails to meet their payment obligations, the **creditor** has the right to claim their portion of the property to cover the unpaid debt. It's important to note that liens always pose a challenge to a **clear title**. -->
If someone who has a loan secured by a lien fails to meet their payment obligations, the **creditor** has the right to claim their portion of the property to cover the unpaid debt. Liens always pose a challenge to a **clear title**.

## Types of Liens

Liens fall into two categories:

1. **Voluntary Liens**: Set up through an agreement, such as a mortgage.
2. **Involuntary Liens**: Imposed by law, such as unpaid taxes.

Other common sources of liens include:

- **Mechanic's Liens**: Result from work performed on the property.
- **Judgment Liens**: Arise from legal judgments against the property owner.

## Impact of Liens on Property Transfer

- A lien does *not* change the title nor stop the owner from transferring their interest in the property.
- However, a property under a lien may be less attractive to potential buyers.
- Once a lien is properly set up, it **attaches** to the property and remains until the debt is cleared.
- If the lien is not cleared before the sale, the new owner inherits the property *with* the lien attached.

## Clearing a Lien: Main Concepts

To clear a lien, three main concepts apply:

1. **Priority**: The lien's place in line compared to other claims.
2. **Satisfaction**: How to settle or pay off the debt.
3. **Enforcement**: How to enforce the terms of the lien agreement.
