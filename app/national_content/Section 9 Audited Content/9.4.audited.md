# Understanding Liens in Title Theory vs. Lien Theory States

When dealing with **liens** in **Title Theory states**, the **right of redemption varies by state**. This contrasts with **Lien Theory states** concerning **mortgages**.

## Differences Between Title Theory and Lien Theory States

- **Lien Theory States:**
  - Borrowers often have a **right of redemption**.
  - This right allows borrowers a final opportunity to reclaim their property by paying off their debt in full, even after the **foreclosure process** has started.

- **Title Theory States:**
  - **The right of redemption after foreclosure varies by state law**.
  - Virginia is a title theory state that provides limited statutory redemption rights in certain situations.

This difference impacts the **rights and options available to borrowers** during and after foreclosure.

Example: In a Lien Theory state, if a borrower defaults on a mortgage, they may still redeem their property by paying the full debt amount before the foreclosure sale is finalized. In Virginia, a Title Theory state, borrowers have limited redemption rights that depend on the specific circumstances and type of foreclosure.
