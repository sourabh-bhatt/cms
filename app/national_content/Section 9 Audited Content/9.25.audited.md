# Mechanic's Lien in Real Estate

<!-- When diving into the world of **liens**, a critical concept you'll encounter is the **Mechanic's Lien**. This type of lien is quite specialized and operates under **statutory guidelines**. It is essentially an **involuntary lien** that can be placed on **real property**. -->
The **Mechanic's Lien** is a specialized type of lien that operates under **statutory guidelines**. It is an **involuntary lien** that can be placed on **real property**.

## Who Can Place a Mechanic's Lien?

- In Virginia, **contractors, subcontractors, laborers, and material suppliers** who provide labor or materials for improvements, repairs, or maintenance to real property can place such a lien.

## What Does a Mechanic's Lien Cover?

- Unlike other types of debts secured against a property, a Mechanic's Lien applies only to payment for work and materials that are **permanently integrated** into the building or land.
- This means the work or materials become a **fixture**, something that cannot be removed without damaging the property.

## Timing of a Mechanic's Lien

- The effective date of a Mechanic's Lien varies by state.
- In Virginia, a mechanic's lien must be filed within 90 days after the last day of the month in which the claimant last performed work or furnished materials.
- Virginia requires contractors to provide written notice to property owners before filing a mechanic's lien.
- <!-- This date is crucial as it influences many subsequent **legal timelines** and rights. -->
- This date influences subsequent **legal timelines** and rights.

## Priority of Mechanic's Liens

- In Virginia, mechanic's liens have priority from the date work began or materials were first furnished, even if filed later.
- This priority can affect other liens and mortgages on the property.

## Impact on Property Transactions

- Mechanic's Liens can be filed even after a property sale is settled.
- Some states require that potential buyers be notified about this possibility through a clause in the **sales contract**.
- This notification ensures buyers are aware that a lien could be placed on their new property for work done prior to their ownership.
- This protects the rights of those who contributed labor or materials to the property.
