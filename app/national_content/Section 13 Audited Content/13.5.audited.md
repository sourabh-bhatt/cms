# Foreign Investment and Real Property Tax Act (FIRPTA) in U.S. Real Estate Transactions

<!-- When you step into the world of real estate, understanding the intricacies of how **foreign investment** impacts property transactions is crucial. **Foreign investment** impacts property transactions through specific U.S. tax laws aimed at foreign persons buying or selling U.S. real estate. The **Foreign Investment and Real Property Tax Act**, commonly known as **FIRPTA**, governs these transactions. This federal law ensures that the U.S. can tax property transfers by foreign owners.

As the global market expands and foreign ownership of U.S. real estate increases, real estate professionals handle transactions involving foreign individuals. These transactions serve various purposes - part-time residence, investment, or business operations in the U.S. Tax rules for foreign persons differ substantially from those for U.S. residents, especially concerning ownership and sale of U.S. real estate.

## Understanding U.S. Real Property Interest (USRPI)

FIRPTA specifically targets what is defined as a **"U.S. real property interest" (USRPI)**. This term encompasses more than just land; it includes:

- Buildings
- Mines
- Wells
- Timber

To prevent foreign individuals from bypassing FIRPTA regulations by incorporating their U.S. real estate holdings, a USRPI also covers any stake in a U.S. corporation, provided the corporation is a **"U.S. real property holding company" (USRPHC)**.

### U.S. Real Property Holding Company (USRPHC)

A corporation is considered a USRPHC if its U.S. real property interests represent **50 percent or more of its total assets**, including both domestic and international real estate as well as other business assets. <!-- This is important because if a foreign investor sells stock in a USRPHC, the transaction may be subject to U.S. federal income tax under FIRPTA. -->
When a foreign investor sells stock in a USRPHC, the transaction may be subject to U.S. federal income tax under FIRPTA.

## Withholding Tax Regime on Purchases from Foreign Sellers

When buying U.S. real property from a foreign seller, there is a crucial withholding tax requirement:

- The purchaser is generally required to **withhold 15% of the gross purchase price**.
- This withheld amount must be sent to the IRS within **20 days following the closing** of the sale.

### Exceptions for Personal Residences

- For personal residences valued at **$300,000 or less**, **no withholding is required**.
- For personal residences valued between **$300,001 and $1 million**, only **10%** of the gross purchase price needs to be withheld.

### Important Notes on Purchase Price

- The **purchase price** includes not only the cash exchanged but also the **fair market value of any other property given** to acquire the real estate.
- Failure to withhold the required amount makes the purchaser responsible for the unpaid taxes, including potential **penalties and interest charges**.
