# Federal Laws Impacting Real Estate Loans Secured by a Principal Dwelling

Real estate professionals must understand federal laws that impact loan management, especially when loans are secured by a principal dwelling. One key piece of legislation is the **Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA)**, enacted in 1994 as an extension of the **Truth in Lending Act (TILA)**.

## Overview of HOEPA

HOEPA regulates a variety of loans, including:

- Most **closed-end home equity mortgages**
- **Home equity lines of credit (HELOCs)**
- **Purchase money mortgages**
- **Refinances**

HOEPA provides extra consumer protections for "high cost" loans.

## Exemptions from HOEPA

Some transactions are exempt from HOEPA:

- **Reverse mortgages**
- Most **construction loans**
- Loans originated and financed by a **housing finance agency**
- Loans under the **Rural Housing Service's Direct Loan Program**

## Disclosure Requirements for HOEPA Loans

HOEPA requires lenders to give borrowers specific written disclosures **at least three business days before** the loan is finalized or the account is opened. These disclosures include:

- A simplified **TILA statement** clarifying that borrowers are not obligated to proceed just because they signed an application or received disclosures.
- Information that securing a loan places a **mortgage on their home**, and defaulting could result in losing the home.
- For **variable-rate loans**, disclosure of the **maximum possible monthly payment** under the loan terms.

## Prohibited Actions by Lenders Under HOEPA

HOEPA prohibits these lender practices:

- Offering loans with **balloon payments** (except under specific conditions).
- Providing **negatively amortizing loans** or loans with **non-amortizing payment schedules**.
- Charging **higher interest rates upon default**.
- Imposing **prepayment penalties** after three years that exceed 2% of the prepaid amount.
- Applying **due-on-demand features** except in particular situations.
- Offering less favorable **refunds of interest following a default**.

## Additional Prohibitions to Preserve Loan Integrity

Lenders are also prohibited from:

- Selling or assigning a loan without informing the borrower.
- Refinancing a HOEPA loan within the first 12 months unless it benefits the borrower.
- Extending credit without considering the borrower's **ability to repay**.
- Extending credit before confirming **homeownership counseling attendance**.
- Advising borrowers to **default on existing loans**.
- Embedding **discount points and fees** into the loan amount.
- Charging fees for **extending, modifying, or renewing** the loan.
- Imposing **excessive late fees**.
- Structuring a loan specifically to **evade HOEPA's regulations**.

## State-Level Laws

Many states and localities have enacted laws that mirror or expand on HOEPA's protections. These may include:

- Further restrictions on **home purchase and equity lines of credit**.
- Limiting or banning **prepayment penalties** and **balloon payments**.
- Prohibiting inclusion of **credit life or other insurance premiums** into the mortgage.
- Mandating borrower participation in **loan counseling**.
